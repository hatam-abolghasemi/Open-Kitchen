# Open Kitchen

*A transparent, self-governing management model for DevOps teams of any size.*

DevOps exists to break the silo between development and operations. But as a DevOps team grows, it splits into fixed groups that rebuild the same silos inside DevOps. Managing them from above adds politics; leaving them alone adds chaos.

Open Kitchen sees the whole tech organization as one kitchen. Product teams cook the dishes, the apps users get, and every dish passes through the same stations on its way to guests: data, security, observability, delivery, orchestration, networking. These stations are the counters, and the DevOps team is the brigade that runs them, side by side with specialists from other teams. Every chef works several counters, and the set keeps changing. The chefs of each counter write its rules, publish their work every week, and are judged by everyone watching. The brigade also looks after its own needs, tests better and cheaper ways of working, and reports its value in the terms the business uses: money, time, risk and customer experience.

Open Kitchen governs the brigade only. Product teams keep organizing themselves their own way.

Open Kitchen uses semantic versions. Brigade records and charters pin the model version they run. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#versioning) and [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).
